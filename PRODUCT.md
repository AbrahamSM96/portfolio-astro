# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers doing fast triage (seconds to form an impression), senior engineers doing a deeper read, and occasional collaborators. Primary audience scans quickly — first impact determines whether they keep reading.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio for Abraham Serrano, Front End Developer. Exists to demonstrate technical mastery and taste through the medium itself: the site IS the proof. Success = a recruiter remembers this one among all the others they saw that day.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, precise, ambitious. The energy of a craftsperson who knows exactly what they're doing and isn't afraid to show it. Dark-field confidence — not aggressive, just authoritative.

## Anti-references

Classic/template portfolios: centered hero photo + name + tagline, grid of cards with same-size thumbnails, pastel or generic SaaS-cream color schemes, cookie-cutter timelines. Anything that could have been generated from a Webflow template. Portfolios that talk about craft but don't demonstrate it.

## Design Principles

1. **Motion as proof** — Animations aren't decoration. Every transition and reveal demonstrates front-end craft directly. If the effect could be on any portfolio, it's not on this one.
2. **Recruiter memory** — 7 seconds to earn 7 minutes. The first scroll must feel different enough to stop the tab-close reflex.
3. **Clarity survives spectacle** — Name, role, and contact must be legible even at peak visual intensity. Effects cannot obscure signal.
4. **Restraint creates impact** — Not every element moves. The contrast between static and kinetic creates the moments that land.
5. **Technical proof in the medium** — A front-end developer's portfolio should do things other portfolios can't. The implementation IS the portfolio item.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. `prefers-reduced-motion` must disable or substitute all kinetic effects with a beautiful static alternative — never just turn off without offering something in return. Color contrast ratios must hold in both full-motion and reduced-motion states.
